CoCoNet

Convex Conformal Network for Multi-dimensional Uncertainty Regions

Alireza Moradi1,3  ·  Thomas Bruys2,3  ·  Pascal Van Hentenryck1,3  ·  Reza Zandehshahvar1,3
1H. Milton Stewart School of Industrial and Systems Engineering, Georgia Tech   2School of Electrical and Computer Engineering, Georgia Tech
3NSF AI Institute for Advances in Optimization (AI4OPT)

arXiv PDF Code Demo BibTeX
Overview. CoCoNet learns a conformity score that is convex in the residual r = y − ŷ from held-out data, calibrates its threshold τ̂α with split conformal prediction, and predicts the convex region {r : Sθ(x, r) ≤ τ̂α} around the forecast.
TL;DR. Instead of fixing the shape of a multivariate conformal region (a box, a ball, an ellipsoid), CoCoNet learns it: a convex score trained to make regions small, followed by split-conformal calibration so that coverage is guaranteed in finite samples.

Abstract

Conformal prediction (CP) provides distribution-free coverage guarantees under exchangeability, but the efficiency of its prediction regions depends critically on the choice of the conformity score. This is particularly important in multivariate regression, where dependencies across output dimensions induce complex joint uncertainty structures. This paper introduces CoCoNet (Convex Conformal Network) for learning convex conformity scores that induce flexible, data-adaptive prediction regions. CoCoNet learns the score from held-out data by optimizing prediction-region efficiency while enforcing convexity with respect to the residual. CoCoNet then applies split-conformal calibration to retain finite-sample coverage guarantees. The paper presents two variants of the method: CoCoNetSpline, based on piecewise-linear scores, and CoCoNetICNN, based on Input Convex Neural Networks. Experiments compare CoCoNet with other CP baselines over synthetic and real-world datasets with output dimensions ranging from 2 to 16. Across 8 models and 30 multi-dimensional regression tasks, CoCoNetICNN and CoCoNetSpline-ℓ₁ achieve the best efficiencies with average ranks of 1.45 and 2.75, respectively. In particular, CoCoNetICNN ranks first on 22 tasks out of 30 in terms of efficiency.

How it works

1Learn
Train a score Sθ(x, r), convex in the residual r, to make the prediction region as small as possible at the target coverage.
2Calibrate
On a separate calibration set, take the conformal quantile τ̂α of the scores. This gives coverage ≥ 1 − α with no distributional assumptions.
3Predict
The region {r : Sθ(x, r) ≤ τ̂α} is a convex sublevel set, so membership and volume are easy to compute and use downstream.

Two score families are studied. CoCoNetICNN uses an input-convex neural network, which can represent smooth convex geometries of any shape. CoCoNetSpline rotates the residual, applies a convex piecewise-linear score to each coordinate and aggregates them with an ℓ₁ or ℓ∞ norm, giving polygonal regions.

CoCoNetSpline. A learned rotation z = Qᵀr, convex piecewise-linear scores G1, G2 per coordinate, and an ℓ₁ or ℓ∞ aggregation, which induce polygonal prediction regions.
